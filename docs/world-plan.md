# World plan: spaces beyond the forge

Working notes for the spaces after the forge island. Decisions marked **Decided** come from me; everything else is a proposal or an open question. The site will keep growing, so the aim at launch is to cover a good spread of interests, not to finish every space. Detailed placement happens with the dev placement tool, space by space.

An ode to everything I'm interested in, built so I can't be put in one box (software engineer, artist, illustrator, and more). Each station will eventually link to a write-up.

## The core idea: every region is a side of me (**Decided**)

The portfolio shows as many sides of me as there are regions, each in its own theme. The forge is the maker. The harbour town is the community and the world. The war side is the raw, gritty, ferocious one. The cave is the private inner one.

## The spaces

### Harbour town: **Decided**
- A community space, not a personal one: half land, half water.
- **Land:** fantasy-town houses (assembled like the cottage), market stalls, a fountain square, a windmill, and some castle elements (walls, towers) from the castle kit.
- **Water:** docks (pirate kit), sailboats, fishing boats, a tug, boathouses, buoys and a large ship (watercraft kit). The clear-water rendering is this space's showpiece.
- **Travel:** open. Candidates are the rowing boat at the forge's jetty, or a train from the Undercroft works (see below), or both.

### Undercroft works: **Decided**
- The factory, industrial city and train kits, plus survival-kit pieces reused from the forge.
- The forge exports materials. Trains are loaded and leave through a tunnel to somewhere unseen (or to the harbour town).
- A trapdoor near the furnace, or a door, or a differently styled cave, leads in.
- The packs are modern next to the forge. That's fine area by area: the contrast is part of the point.
- **Where it sits:** open (see "Flip side or not").

### Cave space (18+): **Decided as an inner area**
- An interior behind the cave mouth on the forge's cliff, reached only through it, never visible by orbiting the island. 18+ opt-in gate with a content note, noindex pages, suggestive work only.
- **Inside:** a dungeon-like open-air theatre (backstage and theatre feel), possibly mixed with a hot-spring grotto. Both appeal.
- **Built from:** the modular cave kit (rock corridors, rooms, stairs, gates, a ladder) and the furniture kit (sofas, lounge chairs, beds, rugs, lamps, a bathtub, kitchen, books, plants), plus a few pieces from the mini-arcade and survival kits. **Decided.** The isometric dungeon pack is out.

### The war side ("Isengard"): **Decided**
The raw and gritty side of me: ferocious, ready to go to war on the problems and issues of the world. One region combining three parts:
- **Castle:** the ideologies and beliefs I defend. Castle-kit walls, towers and gates, armed with siege engines (trebuchets, ballistas, catapults, a siege tower, a ram), and fantasy-town and survival pieces where they help.
- **Graveyard:** memory and remembrance (inspirations, famous people, what I keep). The graveyard kit's mausoleums and crypts.
- **Arena:** the mini-arena pack (sandy floor, stepped stone, columns, statues). Needs sand, so the region blends grass into a sandy fighting ground.
- **Mood reference: Isengard.** A ring-walled stronghold with a tower at its heart, war machines and industry around it.
- **Where:** the island's flip side, or its own slice of land. Open. The flip side suits a "dark side of the same island" reading, and since it isn't gated, being reachable by orbiting is fine.

## Kenney packs: what goes with what (my survey)

- **Factory kit + isometric miniature dungeon** combine well.
- **Fantasy town + castle + survival kits** combine into castles: walls, towers, gates.
- **Mini arena:** a sandy arena with columns and trees.
- **Graveyard kit:** self-explanatory. The two mausoleums work in other settings if the skull on top is hidden or cropped.
- **Mini market:** an indoor shop (shelves, displays, a till).
- **Mini forest:** forest pieces.
- **Modular cave kit (added 2026-10-22, 40 models):** sandstone cave corridors, rooms, wide halls, stairs, gates (rock, metal bars, overhang), a ladder and floor templates. For the cave interior.
- **Furniture kit (added 2026-10-22, 140 models):** a full home: lounge sofas and chairs, beds, rugs, lamps, bathroom, kitchen, desks, a computer, books, plants. Previews are in its `Isometric` and `Side` folders, models in `Models/GLTF format`. For the cave interior, and possibly the town's interiors.
- **Trapdoor:** no model is named as one. Candidates: fantasy-town `planks-opening` (a plank floor with an opening), pirate `hole`, or simple shapes. To confirm with the catalog.
- **Isometric miniature dungeon: not used (decided).** It's 2D, not 3D. Each of its 72 pieces is a pre-rendered image in four directions (N, E, S, W), with lighting baked in. Ways to use it:
  1. **As the visual reference**, building the theatre in 3D from the mini-arena (stepped stone for seating, columns, statues), castle walls and fantasy-town stone. Consistent with everything else, and the camera can still orbit.
  2. **As real 2D for the cave interior only**, with the camera locked to the four directions the images were drawn for (90° turns). The look shifts on entering, which could be the "you've stepped into another world" moment, but it won't match the toon-shaded 3D outside.
  3. **Mixed:** 3D structure with 2D details. Likely to look off; not recommended.

## Open questions

1. **Flip side, inner areas or more slices of land?** My recommendation, one of each:
   - **The flip side holds the Undercroft works.** The island's underside is already a large rock mass hanging below; trains could run out of tunnels in it. If visitors get more camera freedom (wanted), they can orbit underneath anyway, so the underside isn't secret: fine for the works, wrong for 18+ content. Flipping only changes the view while you're underneath; the top stays as it is.
   - **Inner area: the cave space.** Gated and not visible from outside.
   - **A new slice of land: the harbour town.**
   - (Since then the arena and graveyard have joined the castle as one war-side region; see above.)
2. **How to travel between spaces:** boat, train, trapdoor, cave mouth. Each needs a camera transition, built once and shared.
3. **How much more camera control visitors get:** for example a wider tilt. This decides how hidden the underside can be.
4. **Where the war side sits:** the flip side or its own slice of land. Notes on the flip (2026-10-22):
   - **Load:** a flip side doesn't add to the first load. The forge loads and starts rendering first; the other side's models then load in the background while the visitor looks around. Until it's flipped, the other side isn't drawn, so it costs nothing per frame. If someone flips before it has loaded, the turn waits with a small progress cue, or the flip control appears only once it's ready.
   - **No clash:** only one side is ever shown. Each side has a plain rock underside while it's on top, and the swap happens at the moment the island is edge-on during the turn. So the castle never hangs below the forge, and the two sides don't even need the same outline.
   - **Room:** about the forge's footprint (~39 units across), a little more if needed. Enough for a castle ring with the graveyard and arena around it, but tighter than a separate slice of land.
   - **Flip, not camera unlock:** the island turns over, so the war side ends up on top under the same sun and camera rules. Unlocking the camera to look underneath doesn't work for this, because the other side would be upside down and lit from behind. A free-look camera for visitors can still exist as a separate feature.
   - **Cost:** the hanging rock cone gives way to a shallower rock underside on each side, so the silhouette changes a little.

## Technical notes for later

- Each space should load only when entered, so the first visit stays light (the model budget is ~5 MB; the forge alone is 1.3 MB).
- The terrain needs surface types per region (grass, sand, quiet grass) for blending, extending today's ground-patch system.
- Entrances are clickable, so the spaces come after Phase 5 (Interaction).
